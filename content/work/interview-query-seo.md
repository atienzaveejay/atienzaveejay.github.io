## **Context**

When I joined Interview Query, content was already the main acquisition channel, driven primarily by SEO: about ~200,000 clicks per quarter from organic search, roughly ~$40,000/month in revenue, with strong on-page content but weak off-page authority. On paper, things looked solid. In reality, growth had stalled.

### The core problems:

There was an **SEO plateau**, so traffic and revenue weren’t growing. **Conversion efficiency was low**: high traffic, but not all of it monetized. **Authority signals were weak**, with limited off-page presence. And there was an **emerging threat**: AI search (AEO) reducing clicks via direct answers. We weren’t just competing on Google anymore. We were competing on **where people get answers**.

---

## **Insight: Not All Traffic Is Equal**

The biggest shift came from analyzing where revenue was actually coming from. High-traffic content like **SQL, Python, and general tutorials** drove volume, but **company- and role-specific queries** drove conversions. “SQL interview questions” pulled high traffic and low conversion, while “Amazon Data Scientist interview questions” pulled lower traffic but **high purchase intent**. At the same time, YouTube users showed high intent and conversion likelihood, and niche companies like consulting firms had less competition but strong demand.

### Key realization:

> Content should not be optimized for traffic.
> It should be optimized for **intent tied to revenue**.

---

## **Strategy: Build a Content System Around Intent**

We shifted from general content production to a **revenue-first content system**.

### 1. Prioritize high-intent content

We focused heavily on company interview guides (Google, Amazon, and similar), role-specific queries (Data Scientist, Analyst, and similar), and industry-specific niche companies like DoorDash, BCG, and Bain. Instead of chasing volume, we chased **conversion potential** and **search intent depth**.

---

### 2. Build structured funnels, not isolated pages

We designed a clear path:

**Search / Reddit / YouTube → Company Guide → Question Page → Signup → Paid**

- **Company Interview Guide** = entry point (SEO landing page)

- **Question Pages** = conversion engine (interactive product experience)

This structure allowed users to:

- Discover → Explore → Experience → Convert

---

### 3. Expand programmatically, refine strategically

We scaled aggressively, from ~10,000 pages to **30,000+ pages**. Not all pages were equal, so we layered quality by tier:

- Top 100 companies → deeply optimized

- Top 100–500 → solid coverage

- Long tail → SEO-driven, lighter content

We also continuously refreshed high-performing pages every 3–6 months and pruned whatever underperformed.

---

### 4. Use data to guide every decision

We combined **Google Search Console** for real performance data with **Ahrefs** for opportunity discovery, then prioritized pages accordingly: high impressions with low clicks meant optimizing metadata, proven traffic potential meant improving content depth, and revenue potential meant prioritizing aggressively.

---

## **Distribution: Turning Content Into Authority**

We didn’t rely on SEO alone. We built **distribution loops** that reinforced authority.

---

### Reddit (High impact channel)

We used Reddit as both a traffic source and an authority builder: answering real user questions in relevant subreddits, referencing Interview Query naturally, and publishing original research from internal data. A single post could bring up to **20,000 clicks in 2 weeks**, with multiple posts reaching thousands of upvotes. This created a flywheel: Reddit visibility → brand trust → more clicks; Reddit mentions → stronger SEO signals; stronger SEO → more traffic. Domain authority climbed from **35 → 45**.

---

### YouTube (High intent channel)

We tested multiple formats. Mock interviews worked initially but declined over time, while research-driven content proved most scalable. Top videos hit ~300,000 views, with research content consistently pulling 10K+ views. Educational content works, but **market insights and research** are what drive broader reach.

---

### LinkedIn

The highest-performing content here was job market insights, data-backed research, and company-specific breakdowns, content that reinforced brand authority across every other platform.

---

## **Conversion: Turning Visitors Into Revenue**

Most content strategies fail here. We focused heavily on conversion mechanics.

---

### 1. Product-led conversion

Users landed on company guides for information and question pages for interaction. On question pages, they could solve problems, run code, and actually experience the product. That created natural conversion pressure without a hard sell.

---

### 2. Smart gating

We tested multiple triggers and found success with limiting usage (two free code runs, for example) before prompting signup. It worked because users were already engaged, and value had been proven before we asked for anything.

---

### 3. Strategic pop-ups

We implemented scroll-based pop-ups at the 50% mark and delayed prompts. Even with a slight UX trade-off, the conversion gains outweighed the drop-offs.

---

### 4. Post-signup monetization

After signup, users entered email flows, and many converted only after a delay of several days.

> Conversion is not always immediate.
> Engagement compounds over time.

## **Results**

Over my tenure:

- **$569K+ attributable revenue** (Google Analytics)

- Estimated additional ~$500K unattributed revenue (Stripe + other channels)

- Revenue scaled from:
  - ~$40K/month → **$70K–$80K/month peak**

Traffic:

- ~200K → **300K+ clicks per quarter**

Channel contribution:

- ~70% SEO

- ~10% Reddit

- ~10% YouTube

- Remaining from LinkedIn, Bing, AI platforms

---

### Key wins

- Niche content (e.g. consulting firms like BCG, Bain, McKinsey)
  → higher conversion, lower competition

- Reddit research posts
  → massive traffic spikes + SEO lift

- Company interview guides
  → core revenue drivers

---

## **Takeaway**

Most teams optimize content for:

- traffic

- trends

- intuition

What actually works:

> Growth comes from identifying **high-intent opportunities in your data** and building content systems around them.

At Interview Query, we didn’t win by creating more content.

We won by:

- Choosing the right content

- Structuring it into a funnel

- Distributing it strategically

- And optimizing for revenue, not just clicks
